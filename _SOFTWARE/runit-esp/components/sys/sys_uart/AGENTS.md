# sys_uart

Spec: `SYS_UART.MD` (this folder).

- Every change in this folder is followed by an edit of this file, if it is needed.
- The id CREATE is given, the providers' state (wired, framed) and `sys_uart_cfg_t` (CREATE's frame), `sys_uart_mode_e` and `sys_uart_parity_e` are wire format: the app and the scripts name them. Never renumber; a new mode or a field is added in `sys_uart_router.h` / `sys_uart_config.h`, `contract_uart.json` and `SYS_UART.MD` together, then `templates/sync.sh`.
- The line format of a framed port is wire format: `00 cobs([frame][crc16 LE]) 00`, CRC16-CCITT (poly 0x1021, init 0xFFFF). `router_link.py` (the scripts) encodes and decodes the same: change both together.
- One UART is one provider, with one reader (its rx hook). Never a second provider on a UART (two readers would split its RX bytes); what a line must carry at once is a framed port with one session, and a device's own protocol is a raw port of its own.
- All ports but the console's are made by one function (`open_port`): CREATE and the link (Kconfig) differ only in the pins' source and `STATE_SYSTEM`. Their memory is the heap's, taken at create and freed by the provider's `destroy` (`release_port`), in the order it was made; a failure at any step releases what was made. The console's tasks and rings are static (`U_*_DEFINE`).
- A task of a made port ends only through `task_out` after `stop` (`stop_tasks`): the port's memory is freed after both are out (or deleted after `STOP_WAIT_MS`). A task never touches the port after `task_out`.
- CREATE's frame is checked once, in `sys_uart_create`; `open_port` and below trust it. The link's pins come from Kconfig and bypass the pin-device check (the GPIO device does not exist yet at `sys_uart_init`).
- Static buffers: each port (`port_t`) has its candidate buffers (a candidate is COBS-decoded in place; a raw port's message is collected in the same one) and its TX task's line, sized for `CMD_FRAME_MAX` (`cmd.h`). A queued item is `[frame][crc16]` (a raw port's: the bytes): `send` adds the CRC, so the TX task encodes the item as it is. The ports share nothing but the COBS and CRC functions and `sys_uart_lock` (the table of made ports); a task takes its port as its argument.
- The link (`CONFIG_SYS_UART_LINK`) is a port with no USB output and a TX task that waits for room; its pins and rate are Kconfig, its rate the console's (the other board's console UART is its far end). Its pins must stay clear of the console's, the USB's (19/20), the strapping pins (0, 3, 45, 46), the flasher's wires and the board's I2C and expander pins.
- The link is never removed. Lent (`sys_uart_link_direct`), it is the same port in DIRECT mode with its tasks stopped (`stop_tasks`) and its provider down; taken back, `start_tasks` makes its tasks again and the provider goes up. Only sys_flasher lends it, on one task.
- A DIRECT port has no tasks, no rings and `STATE_NO_RX`: nothing may assume `tx_task` or a ring when `mode` says so (`release_port` skips what was not made). Nothing may read it but its owner, by the id, on its own task.
- `sys_uart_transact` keeps its answer's state in one static struct under `s_transact_lock`, never on the caller's stack: the provider's `deliver` may still call its rx after `close`, and a wait that has ended takes nothing.
- Errors are `ERR_MOD_SYS_UART` (24), CREATE's and the raw command's; a send's failure is the provider's (`ERR_PROV_BUSY`, raised here). Each tag has its text in `errors/errors_sys_uart.json`.
- TX: the provider's `send` only queues (normal or urgent ring) and notifies the TX task; only the TX task writes frames. The console's write to the UART never blocks on its baud rate (the driver's TX buffer; a frame that does not fit is not written there); a made port's driver has no TX buffer, its TX task writes to the FIFO and waits; the USB-Serial-JTAG gets pieces of at most half its TX buffer.
- RX: a candidate that is no frame is dropped silently (no report: the ROM's text and a panic are no frames). Valid frames, and a raw port's messages, go to the provider's user (`sys_data_provider_deliver`).
- Initialization: `sys_uart_init` installs the console drivers, redirects `stdout` VFS to the UART driver, adds the provider and launches the TX and RX tasks, then makes the link; the session attaches them afterwards (`runit.c`). `runit.c` registers `sys_uart_router` under `ROUTER_KEY_UART`.
